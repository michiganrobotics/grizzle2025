  <section class="section" id="students">
    <h2>Start engineering mathematics with theory you can use</h2>
    <div class="split center">
      <div>
        <p class="section-intro">ROB 101 puts linear algebra and programming together from the beginning without reducing the mathematics to software training. Students build a working command of systems of equations, linear independence, span, bases, rank, the rank-nullity theorem, eigenvalues, eigenvectors, factorization, least squares, orthogonality, null spaces, root finding, and optimization.</p>
        <p>The computational setting lets the course ask more of students, not less. Small hand calculations establish definitions and proofs; Julia assignments then make the same ideas operate on hundreds or thousands of variables. Matrices become both mathematical objects and engineering tools for perception, regression, classification, and control.</p>
        <div class="qa-strip">
          <div class="qa">
            <h3>Who should take it?</h3>
            <p>First-year students interested in robotics, AI, data, computer vision, machine learning, engineering, or applied mathematics.</p>
          </div>
          <div class="qa">
            <h3>Fall 2026 format</h3>
            <p>The remote section will be closed in Fall 2026 after students overused AI. The course is expected to reopen a remote pathway in a future semester once assessment uses the North Campus computer-based testing center powered by PrairieLearn.</p>
          </div>
          <div class="qa">
            <h3>Why ROB 101 instead of waiting?</h3>
            <p>Linear algebra is central to robotics, machine learning, computer vision, and data. ROB 101 lets students meet that language early, with both theory and applications attached.</p>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

  <section class="section compact" id="preparation">
    <h2>Theoretical preparation, computationally grounded</h2>
    <p class="section-intro">The textbook is explicit about the preparation ROB 101 provides. Students learn the mathematics of linear systems and vector spaces alongside the algorithms that make those ideas reliable at scale.</p>
    <div class="project-rows">
      <div class="project-row">
        <div class="project-body">
          <p class="project-num">Linear systems</p>
          <h3>From Ax = b to factorization</h3>
          <p>Students study systems of equations, determinants, triangular systems, forward and back substitution, matrix multiplication, permutation matrices, inverses, and LU factorization for solving linear equations.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="project-media">
          <span class="formula-line">Ax = b</span>
          <p>The course emphasizes what happens when systems have one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions, and how structure makes large systems computable.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="project-row project-row--flip">
        <div class="project-body">
          <p class="project-num">Vector spaces</p>
          <h3>Geometry, subspaces, and orthogonality</h3>
          <p>Students learn linear combinations, span, linear independence, bases, coordinates, dimension, eigenvalues, eigenvectors, rank, nullity, the rank-nullity theorem, column spaces, null spaces, dot products, orthonormal vectors, Gram-Schmidt, and QR factorization.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="project-media">
          <span class="formula-line">A = QR</span>
          <p>This is the theoretical core behind regression, projection, perception, and many later topics in machine learning and control.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="project-row">
        <div class="project-body">
          <p class="project-num">Numerical methods</p>
          <h3>Approximation, root finding, and optimization</h3>
          <p>Students use least-squares regression, bisection, numerical derivatives, Newton and Newton-Raphson methods, gradient descent, Hessians, and introductory quadratic programming and max-margin classification.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="project-media">
          <span class="formula-line">arg min f(x)</span>
          <p>Optimization is treated as a mathematical idea and as a practical engine for modern engineering systems.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="section compact" id="projects">
    <h2>Projects at the scale of life</h2>
    <p class="section-intro">The projects are where preparation turns into engineering judgment. They are not a substitute for theory; they are the place where the theory has to work.</p>
    <div class="ideas">
      <div class="idea">
        <h3>LiDAR mapping</h3>
        <p>Students process point-cloud measurements, coordinate frames, and matrix-vector operations to build a map a robot can use for navigation.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="idea">
        <h3>Regression and machine learning</h3>
        <p>Students fit models to a large NOAA precipitation dataset using least squares and radial basis functions.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="idea">
        <h3>Segway control</h3>
        <p>Students connect matrices, optimization, ODEs, and discrete-time models to balancing and driving a mobile robot.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="idea">
        <h3>Camera and LiDAR models</h3>
        <p>Students encounter pinhole cameras, homogeneous coordinates, calibration, and projections between sensors.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="section compact" id="ideas">
    <h2>The organizing ideas</h2>
    <p class="section-intro">ROB 101 becomes memorable because the same mathematical moves keep reappearing in different settings.</p>
    <div class="ideas">
      <div class="idea"><h3>Representation</h3><p>Vectors and matrices give engineers a compact language for equations, images, point clouds, signals, and robot states.</p></div>
      <div class="idea"><h3>Solvability</h3><p>Systems of equations can have one solution, no solution, or many. Students learn how structure determines what is possible.</p></div>
      <div class="idea"><h3>Factorization</h3><p>LU and QR reveal the structure inside matrices, turning hard systems into organized sequences of simpler problems.</p></div>
      <div class="idea"><h3>Approximation</h3><p>Least squares, regression, projection, and classification let students make principled models when exact answers are not available.</p></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="section" id="evidence">
    <h2>The ROB 101 precedent matters</h2>
    <p class="section-intro">ROB 101 was created in 2020 in anticipation of the Michigan Robotics undergraduate major launching in 2022. It began as a 41-student Fall 2020 pilot and now enrolls roughly 150 to 200 students in both fall and winter terms.</p>
    <div class="charts">
      <div class="chart">
        <h3>Students reach advanced courses earlier</h3>
        <p>ROB 101 students reached machine-learning coursework about two semesters earlier than students who took the traditional MATH 214 route.</p>
        <img src="/images/preview/rob101-earlier-ml.png" alt="Comparison showing ROB 101 students reaching machine learning earlier than students from the MATH 214 path">
      </div>
      <div class="chart">
        <h3>Downstream outcomes are comparable</h3>
        <p>In EECS 442 Computer Vision and EECS 445 Machine Learning, grade outcomes for ROB 101 and MATH 214 students were statistically comparable.</p>
        <img src="/images/preview/rob101-grade-outcomes.png" alt="Grade outcome comparison for ROB 101 and MATH 214 students in EECS 442 and EECS 445">
      </div>
    </div>
    <dl class="deflist">
      <div class="deflist-row">
        <dt>Equity</dt>
        <dd>One design goal was to provide a more equitable learning environment for students coming from less well-resourced high schools. Data from the first several terms show grade outcomes that are much more equitable than in many introductory STEM courses.</dd>
      </div>
      <div class="deflist-row">
        <dt>Retention</dt>
        <dd>Overall College of Engineering retention rates, and retention rates for female students, were higher for students who took ROB 101 than for students who did not.</dd>
      </div>
      <div class="deflist-row">
        <dt>Assessment</dt>
        <dd>Michigan Robotics, CRLT, and CRLT-Engin analyzed upper-level courses requiring linear algebra, comparing students who satisfied the prerequisite through ROB 101 with those who took MATH 214. The EEI Days 2026 session credits this work to a team that included Dr. Heather Rypkema, Head of Learning Analytics and Associate Director of the Foundational Course Initiative at CRLT.</dd>
      </div>
    </dl>
    <p class="takeaway"><strong>The implication:</strong> students can move into advanced AI and robotics coursework earlier without losing preparation once they arrive, while the course also advances Michigan Robotics' equity goals.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="band" id="faculty">
    <div class="section">
      <h2>For faculty and potential adopters</h2>
      <p class="section-intro">ROB 101 is open, scalable, and built around a complete ecosystem of textbook chapters, Julia labs, lecture notes, homework, programming assignments, projects, recorded lectures, and a tested large-course staffing model.</p>
      <dl class="deflist">
        <div class="deflist-row">
          <dt>Scale</dt>
          <dd>One instructor with a team of undergraduate teaching assistants can handle 250 students per semester.</dd>
        </div>
        <div class="deflist-row">
          <dt>Access</dt>
          <dd>Algebra is the only prerequisite, and Julia can be used through browser-based course infrastructure rather than expensive software. For Fall 2026, the remote section is being paused while assessment is redesigned around controlled computer-based testing.</dd>
        </div>
        <div class="deflist-row">
          <dt>Theory</dt>
          <dd>The course includes linear systems, LU, determinants, inverses, vector spaces, span, linear independence, bases, eigenvalues, eigenvectors, rank, the rank-nullity theorem, null spaces, Gram-Schmidt, QR, least squares, regression, root finding, gradient descent, Hessians, and classification foundations.</dd>
        </div>
        <div class="deflist-row">
          <dt>Pathway</dt>
          <dd>ROB 101 is the undergraduate entry point for a mathematics sequence that can connect naturally to ROB 201 and ROB 501.</dd>
        </div>
      </dl>
    </div>
  </section>

    <h2>For communications</h2>
    <p class="section-intro">ROB 101 is the story of moving serious linear algebra earlier, making computation part of mathematics from day one, and letting students see robotics and AI as reachable fields in their first year.</p>
    <div class="qa-strip">
      <div class="qa">
        <h3>Public message</h3>
        <p>Michigan Robotics teaches linear algebra through computation, data, robotics, and intelligent systems.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="qa">
        <h3>Why it matters</h3>
        <p>The course gives students an earlier, more motivating path into the mathematical language of robotics and AI.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="qa">
        <h3>Adoption angle</h3>
        <p>ROB 101 has already scaled to large enrollments while keeping course materials open and reusable.</p>
